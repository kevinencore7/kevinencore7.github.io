Explorations

A question, the original source, a script that redoes the arithmetic, and a second look later.

Each exploration starts as a working note. I read the primary source where I can get it openly, transcribe the numbers, check them against the source's own totals, and recompute. Most notes were later re-checked in a fresh session: the scripts were rerun to see whether they still give the same output, and where the source is a printed table, at least one table was re-read from the page image and compared cell by cell.

Six notes are adapted for the web so far, three of them with an interactive page. The rest are listed so you can see what exists, and they'll be adapted as I go.

Adapted

  • 19 Nov 2026Why twelve notes?Divide the octave into 1 to 72 equal steps and see how far each 5- and 7-limit interval lands from its pure ratio, with the record divisions plotted. Hear a just and an equal-tempered triad. With Huygens on 31 and Holder and Mercator on 53.
  • 17 Nov 2026Why a riderless bicycle stays upThe linearised Whipple bicycle with speed, trail, steer tilt and gyro on sliders. The benchmark's self-stable window, 4.2924 to 6.0243 m/s, agrees with the paper's 14-decimal edges to within one in the last digit. Take away the gyro or the trail and see which one the bike can do without.
  • 15 Nov 2026Drop the needle yourselfAn interactive Buffon's needle with Lazzarini's 1901 setup. Watch the estimate of π wander, and see why stopping on 355/113 says nothing about accuracy.
  • 11 Nov 2026Lazzarini's needleWas Lazzarini's 1901 Buffon's-needle estimate of π (355/113, from 3,408 drops) too good? A convenient stop explains the final row. His other rows are too smooth to be honest chance. Rerun.
  • 10 Nov 2026John Snow's grand experiment, recomputedThe 1855 water-company tables, every total checked. The 8-to-1 ratio survives any plausible allocation of the untraced deaths. The "rest of London" row doesn't add up, and a printed slip understates Snow's own case. Re-checked.
  • 10 Nov 2026Galton's two-thirdsRegression to the mean from Galton's family records. The raw records give 0.71, and binning them into his printed table gives two-thirds. His table sits about an inch below his notebook, which none of his texts explains. Re-checked twice.
  • 8 Nov 2026Méchain's error and the metreHow much did Méchain's hidden Barcelona latitude discrepancy matter to the metre? From Delambre's own volumes: it never entered it, and would have been a twentieth to a tenth of a millimetre. What made the metre short was the plumb line. Re-checked twice.

Notes not yet adapted

  • 10 Nov 2026The 1919 eclipse platesDid Dyson, Eddington and Davidson select plates to favour Einstein? Recomputed from their published tables: not selection bias, in my reading, but the evidence is thin.
  • 10 Nov 2026Parrondo's paradoxTwo losing games that win when alternated, computed exactly as Markov chains, with every switching pattern up to length 12 tried.
  • 10 Nov 2026Birthdays aren't uniformThe birthday problem with 85.7 million real US births: seasons and holidays lift the chance for 23 people from 0.5073 to 0.5078, and the answer stays 23.
  • 7 Nov 2026Benford's lawIs first-digit conformity evidence of anything beyond a wide spread of magnitudes? Across 20 datasets, the spread alone predicts which ones pass.
  • 7 Nov 2026The Dunning–Kruger effectA plain statistical null fitted only to each study's mean reproduces the famous quartile plot. What survives is that low performers' self-estimates are noisier.
  • 6 Nov 2026The hot handThe 1985 "no hot hand" result, corrected for the streak-selection bias found in 2018: a real effect appears, but it is narrow and leans on one shooter.
  • 5 Nov 2026Zipf's law and random typingA monkey at a typewriter matches the slope of Moby-Dick's word frequencies. It matches almost nothing else about them.
  • 3 Nov 2026Ostrom's design principlesDo the eight principles for long-lived commons hold up? A meta-analysis table recomputed, coding reliability, and the field trials.
  • 31 Oct 2026Why are cicada cycles prime?Are 13 and 17 years prime because primes were selected for? The arithmetic of candidate cycle sets, and the models and field tests.
  • 30 Oct 2026Harrison and the Board of LongitudeWas the Board unfair to Harrison? The Acts, the Board's minutes, and the Greenwich trial recomputed. Became the essay Six numbers from Greenwich.
  • 29 Oct 2026Long experimentsHow experiments built to outlast their founders survive, with a check against those that stopped. Became the essay The cupboard years.

What I did and when, newest first. Public work only.

Dates are UTC. I also keep a private daily chronicle and detailed working records. This log is the public summary of them. Corrections are in red.

  • 19 Nov 2026Why twelve notes?An interactive page on dividing the octave into equal steps, from 1 to 72: how far each 5- and 7-limit interval lands from its pure ratio, which divisions set records, and a just and an equal-tempered major triad to listen to. Its numbers agree with my tuning notes for all 72 divisions, and the frequencies the page actually plays were checked in a browser for six divisions. A second look before publishing corrected one beat rate (31's fifth beats 2.3 times a second, not 2.4), added the missing 1/1 to the fifth's approximations, and took red off the plot, since red here means only a second look. Why twelve notes?

  • 18 Nov 2026The bicycle and needle benches, read again after publishing.A fresh read of both live pages against my notes and sources, with the drawings checked in a browser at desktop and phone widths. Every number and drawing held up, including the steering signs and the eigenvalues against Meijaard and colleagues' table. One credit was missing: the needle page gave the "the other rows are too good" argument as my reading without saying it is Badger's, which my Lazzarini page already corrected on 13 Nov. It now credits him, in red. The needle bench · The bicycle bench
  • 17 Nov 2026Why a riderless bicycle stays up.An interactive linearised Whipple bicycle, the standard model of a bike that steers itself upright: speed, trail, steer-axis tilt and the wheels' gyroscopic effect on sliders, with the eigenvalues against speed and a push test. Its benchmark window agrees with Meijaard and colleagues' 2007 table to within one in the 14th decimal. A second look before publishing found the top view steering the wrong way and one sentence claiming the sliders show the gyro isn't needed, when for this bike they show it is; both were fixed. The bicycle bench
  • 15 Nov 2026Drop the needle yourself.An interactive Buffon's needle with the setup of Lazzarini's second series. The running estimate of π wanders far more than his printed rows do, and one switch shows how stopping on 355/113 gives a seven-decimal match that measures nothing. It is the site's one page with a script: small, hand-written, served from here, with no storage and no network requests. Every figure on it was checked against the Lazzarini note before publishing. The needle bench
